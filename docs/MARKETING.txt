HTML LONG DESCRIPTION

<h2>A grow log for microgreen trays</h2>

<p>Microgreen Tracker records what you seeded, when, in what size tray, and when you expect to cut it. It is a WordPress plugin, so the log lives in your own database next to everything else on your site — no third-party service and no per-tray fee.</p>

<h3>What the free plugin does</h3>

<ul>
  <li><strong>One tray record.</strong> Variety, tray size, seeded date and expected harvest, stored on an ordinary <code>mgtrack_tray</code> post type.</li>
  <li><strong>Variety as a taxonomy.</strong> Names accumulate as you use them, so a typo does not quietly become a second variety.</li>
  <li><strong>Dates that are actually checked.</strong> A value is validated against the calendar before it is written, and refused with the value quoted back — <code>2026-02-30</code> has the right shape and is not a day.</li>
  <li><strong>Status you cannot contradict.</strong> A tray's status is derived from what has happened to it, never stored as a separate field that can disagree.</li>
  <li><strong>Readable from the command line.</strong> <code>wp post list --post_type=mgtrack_tray</code>.</li>
</ul>

<h3>What Microgreen Tracker Pro adds</h3>

<p>Pro is an add-on. It extends this same tray record rather than keeping its own, so every tray you have already logged is there the moment you install it.</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Racks and locations</strong> — occupancy across shelves and slots, with a full slot refused rather than double-booked.</li>
  <li><strong>Harvest planning</strong> — plan backwards from the date you need the crop.</li>
  <li><strong>Yield and cost reporting</strong> — what each tray produced and what it cost to produce.</li>
  <li><strong>A grow-room role and five front-end shortcodes</strong> — staff log trays and milestones from a phone, without ever seeing wp-admin.</li>
</ul>
